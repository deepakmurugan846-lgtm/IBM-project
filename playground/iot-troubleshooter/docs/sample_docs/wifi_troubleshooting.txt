IoT Device Troubleshooting Guide — WiFi Connectivity
======================================================
Version 2.1 | Generic / Multi-Vendor Reference

1. OVERVIEW
-----------
WiFi connectivity is the most common failure category for consumer IoT devices.
Issues fall into four buckets: (A) initial pairing failure, (B) intermittent
drops after successful connection, (C) inability to reconnect after a reboot,
and (D) reduced range or slow throughput.

2. INITIAL PAIRING FAILURES
----------------------------
Symptom: Device does not appear in the mobile app or setup wizard times out.

Step 1 — Verify band compatibility
Most IoT devices (smart plugs, bulbs, sensors) operate ONLY on the 2.4 GHz
band. If your router broadcasts a single SSID for both 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz
("band steering"), the device may attempt to join 5 GHz and fail.
  Fix: Create a dedicated 2.4 GHz SSID in your router admin panel, or
       temporarily disable 5 GHz during pairing.

Step 2 — Check SSID length and characters
Some devices have a 32-character SSID limit and do not support spaces or
special characters (!, @, #, $, %, etc.) in the password.
  Fix: Simplify your SSID and password to alphanumeric characters only
       during initial setup.

Step 3 — WPA3 / WPA2 mismatch
Older IoT firmware often supports WPA2-PSK (AES) but not WPA3-SAE.
If your router is set to "WPA3 Only" the device will fail the handshake.
  Fix: Set router security to "WPA2/WPA3 Mixed" or "WPA2-PSK (AES)".

Step 4 — DHCP pool exhaustion
If your router's DHCP pool is fully allocated the device will not receive
an IP address and will appear disconnected.
  Fix: Check connected devices in the router admin panel. Increase the
       DHCP range or release stale leases.

Step 5 — Factory reset the device
Hold the reset button for 5–10 seconds (device-specific; consult the label)
until the LED flashes rapidly. This clears any prior network credentials.
⚠️  A factory reset erases all device settings and scene configurations.

3. INTERMITTENT DROPS
---------------------
Symptom: Device connects initially but goes offline periodically (minutes to hours).

Cause 3a — RF interference
Microwave ovens, baby monitors, and Bluetooth speakers operate on 2.4 GHz and
can cause packet loss. Zigbee and WiFi channels can also overlap.
  Fix: Change WiFi channel to 1, 6, or 11 (non-overlapping). Move device
       away from microwave.

Cause 3b — DHCP lease renewal failure
If the router refuses to renew a lease, the device loses its IP.
  Fix: Assign a static IP or DHCP reservation using the device's MAC address.

Cause 3c — Router "client isolation" or AP isolation
Some mesh networks isolate clients from each other, breaking cloud callbacks.
  Fix: Disable AP isolation in the router/mesh settings.

Cause 3d — Firmware power-management bug
Some devices aggressively enter power-save mode and miss keep-alive packets.
  Fix: Check the manufacturer's latest firmware — power-management bugs are
       frequently patched. Update via the device app.

4. REBOOT RECONNECTION ISSUES
------------------------------
Symptom: Device connects fine, but after a power cycle it cannot reconnect.

Step 1 — Confirm stored credentials
If the router SSID or password changed, the device still holds the old
credentials. Re-pair the device.

Step 2 — Order of boot
Bring the router/gateway up fully (≥60 seconds) before powering the IoT
device. Many devices time out during the DHCP negotiation window at boot.

Step 3 — DNS resolution
Some devices phone home to a cloud server during boot. If your router uses
a custom DNS that blocks the manufacturer's domain, the device will show
as offline even when connected to WiFi.
  Fix: Temporarily switch DNS to 8.8.8.8 and test.

5. LED STATUS CODES (generic)
------------------------------
Slow blink (1/sec): Searching for network / not paired
Fast blink (3/sec): Pairing mode active
Solid: Connected and operational
Double blink: Authentication error (wrong password)
3 rapid blinks + pause: IP address not obtained (DHCP failure)
Alternating red/white: Firmware update in progress — do NOT power off
Solid red: Hardware fault or cloud connectivity failure

6. ESCALATION CRITERIA
-----------------------
Escalate to manufacturer support when:
  - All steps above have been attempted without resolution
  - The device shows solid red LED continuously
  - The device is within the 1-year warranty period
  - Physical damage is visible on the device
